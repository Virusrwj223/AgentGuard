0. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Build a governance layer that allows a small business to delegate customer
negotiations to QwenWork agents while independently assessing the economic
consequences of their proposed decisions before they make commitments.

The agent interprets open-ended customer requests and generates candidate
business strategies. A deterministic compiler combines the proposed strategy,
current business state and a human-approved domain knowledge base into a
probabilistic state-transition model. PAT (Process Analysis Toolkit), using
its PCSP module, evaluates the model. A decision engine translates the result
into expected economic utility and authorizes a suitable strategy or abstains.
An audit agent and an explainable AI (XAI) component provide a reviewable
record of the decision, supporting evidence and financial trade-offs.

Core design principle: different business risks are represented as states,
transitions and monetary consequences, NOT as separately generated governance
assertions. The runtime verifier uses ONE FIXED economic-utility query for
all supported negotiations. A new risk should generally extend the approved
domain knowledge, not require a new verification objective.

The engine is intended to be industry-independent; the hackathon MVP is a
travel-agency implementation inspired by Mesho Travel & Tours, an existing
Hashmeta AI CMS customer. Hashmeta reports its first ChatGPT-originated
booking inquiry within 14 days and 29 form submissions by 19 September 2026
[1]. The project investigates the NEXT stage: converting such inquiries into
safely governed commercial decisions. No actual Mesho financial information,
customer incidents or private supplier data are assumed. Demo data are synthetic.

The organizer requires genuine QwenWork platform use (20% of judging) and a
website built with Hashmeta AI (15%) [2]. Both are essential deliverables,
not optional branding. Use QwenWork for the actual agent workflows and
Hashmeta AI CMS for the public product website and demo entry point.

Submission: 23 November 2026; three-minute demo video [2]. The team has
approximately six days from kickoff. This is a prototype, not a claim of
production-grade, comprehensive legal or cybersecurity assurance.

1. TARGET OUTCOME AND SCOPE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Customer problem
A small travel agency receives a request to change an existing booking at
no extra cost. It must reason about supplier availability, amendment fees,
refund obligations, customer retention and potential business losses, but
its founder cannot manually inspect every interaction. Static discount
limits do not capture the trade-offs across possible future outcomes.

1.2 Target behaviour
For a customer request, the system shall:
  a. Interpret the request, resolve the relevant booking and extract goals.
  b. Propose alternative strategies using known operations.
  c. Retrieve trusted business data and approved consequences/probabilities.
  d. Compile each strategy into a bounded probabilistic transition system.
  e. Evaluate the expected utility using a single fixed PAT assertion.
  f. Compare candidate strategies against the applicable baseline and the
     owner's risk appetite; select, counteroffer, reject or escalate.
  g. Execute or communicate only the exact approved commitment.
  h. Record decision provenance and provide an understandable explanation.

1.3 MVP priorities
MUST: One travel agency; a versioned knowledge pack; QwenWork customer-facing
agent; QwenWork-assisted knowledge acquisition; typed intermediate
representation (IR); deterministic compiler; actual PAT PCSP execution; one
utility query; comparison against rejection/status quo; safe execution gate;
structured audit trail; XAI summary; published Hashmeta AI website.

SHOULD: Independent risk-review agent; approved parameter scenarios; review
UI; two or three candidate strategies; replayable audit records; combined
unseen customer requests; automated sensitivity summaries.

COULD: Multiple business contexts; richer uncertainty models; ongoing
knowledge discovery; real supplier/CRM integrations; concurrency; insurance;
advanced planning or policy synthesis. These are not release blockers.

Do not build a general CRM, chatbot platform, booking/payment processor,
unrestricted AI compliance engine or arbitrary natural-language-to-PAT
generator. Simulate supplier operations and commitments for the demo.

2. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND TRUST BOUNDARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  Hashmeta AI CMS website (public product/demo entry)
                       |
                       v
  Customer request -> QwenWork customer / planning agent
                       | structured intent + candidate plan(s)
                       v
  Governance connector (MCP or supported QwenWork tool interface)
                       |
                       v
  Orchestrator -> IR validator -> deterministic compiler -> PAT adapter
                       ^                    ^                   |
                       |                    |                   v
  Trusted business state store      Approved knowledge pack    Utility result
                       |                                        |
                       +-----------> Decision engine <----------+
                                             |
                                  Authorization / execution gate
                                             |
                                    Approved outward response

  Knowledge workflow: QwenWork research agent -> proposed knowledge ->
                      independent review (optional) -> human approval
                      -> immutable version of knowledge pack

  Audit workflow: every stage -> structured event log -> XAI / audit agent
                  -> private founder review dashboard

Trust rules:
- Agent output is untrusted input, not executable code or authoritative data.
- Compiler, model checker adapter, utility and authorization logic run on
  the backend. The agent cannot change their semantics or threshold.
- Live prices, availability, customer identity and approved assumptions are
  retrieved independently from trusted stores. In the MVP, these are mocks.
- Only approved knowledge and supported operation templates enter a model.
- ONLY the guarded executor may make binding offers or update bookings.
- Customer utterances, supplier documents and web pages are untrusted text,
  never instructions capable of modifying policies, tools or credentials.
- External e-mail, payment, write-enabled CRM and booking connectors must
  not be directly available to the sales agent in the MVP.
- Where QwenWork cannot technically enforce output routing, its chat is an
  internal drafting interface only. The backend customer channel displays
  only a guarded result. Never claim stronger enforcement than demonstrated.

4. STEP 2: DEFINE THE DOMAIN-INDEPENDENT DATA CONTRACTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Owner: compiler/backend engineer. Priority: MUST.

4.1 Abstract state (typed, finite model)
State must represent enough information to evaluate downstream consequences,
without storing arbitrary customer language or entire documents in PAT.
Suggested dimensions:
- Finance: incremental revenue, supplier costs, losses, refunds, exposure;
  use signed integer minor currency units, preferably cents.
- Resources: quantities, reservations, capacity, availability/confirmation.
- Commitments: service status, agreed dates, cancellation terms, obligations.
- Payments: amount paid, amount due, payment status, deadlines.
- Time: bounded time buckets or relative days, not an unbounded clock.
- Event status: flags/counters needed to derive subsequent consequences.
- Economic value accumulator; terminal/finished status.

Model only a transaction and bounded relevant context for the MVP. Shared
business liquidity or inventory may be included as read-only inputs, but
avoid claiming portfolio-level guarantees without modelling interactions.
Normalize currency and time on the backend. Validate ranges and reject
arithmetic overflow. Store data version, effective date and provenance.

4.2 Intermediate representation (agent output contract)
The agent returns strictly schema-validated JSON, not CSP#, arbitrary Python,
SQL, executable formulas, raw probability values or authorization commands.
Example (conceptual):

  {
    "request_id": "REQ-001",
    "booking_id": "B001",
    "goal": {"type": "amend_booking", "date_offset_days": 30},
    "customer_constraints": {"max_extra_charge_cents": 0},
    "needs_clarification": false,
    "candidate_strategies": [
      {"strategy_id": "keep_original", "plan": {"op": "KEEP_BOOKING"}},
      {"strategy_id": "conditional_amend", "plan": {
        "op": "SEQUENCE", "steps": [
          {"op": "REQUEST_AMENDMENT", "booking_ref": "B001",
           "date_offset_days": 30},
          {"op": "IF", "condition": {"predicate": "AMENDMENT_APPROVED"},
           "then": {"op": "CONFIRM_AMENDMENT"},
           "else": {"op": "KEEP_BOOKING"}}
        ]}}
    ]
  }

Operation names and condition references are registered identifiers. This
example is a contract sketch; the team should freeze the exact schema on
Day 1. The agent may also suggest a surcharge or alternative date, but an
offer exceeding the customer's constraints must be labelled a counteroffer
requiring consent, not an accepted transaction. Booking ID must be resolved
from the authorized session, not trusted merely because the agent wrote it.

4.3 Plan grammar
MVP: Operation(args), Sequence(steps), Conditional(predicate, then, else).
Allow a contingent fallback based on an observable supplier response. Use a
finite DAG/unrolled plan and cap length, branches and recursion depth. No
arbitrary loops, unbounded retries, arbitrary code or unregistered predicates.
Optional: bounded Choice/Parallel, with explicitly defined scheduler semantics.

4.4 Result contract
Return request/strategy/model IDs; status (APPROVED, COUNTEROFFER, REJECTED,
CLARIFY, NEEDS_REVIEW or ERROR); expected utility or conservative interval;
relative baseline; decision threshold; input/knowledge versions; approved
terms (if any); diagnostics; audit ID; authorization token (executor only).
Use schema validation and canonical serialization on both sides of the tool.

Definition of done: agent-produced JSON validates; malformed, missing,
unsupported or unauthorized requests fail closed; new combinations of valid
operations require no changes to the IR grammar or economic query.

5. STEP 3: BUILD THE TRAVEL DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE PACK
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Owner: knowledge/agent engineer + business reviewer. Priority: MUST.

5.1 Separate engine from data
The compiler core owns composition and target-language semantics. The domain
pack supplies the concrete resource definitions, operation templates, causal
consequences, statistical parameters, valuations, and source information.
Travel assumptions must not be scattered through compiler code or prompts.
A new industry requires a new validated pack and potentially new operations,
not a new utility objective. Generalizability is architectural, not yet
empirically demonstrated across industries.

5.2 Knowledge records
Each assumption: ID, type (fact/statistic/causal link/valuation/preference),
value or range, units, conditional context, source or fixture label, sample
size/time period (if applicable), confidence/limitations, approval status,
approver, effective/expiry date and version. Keep source excerpts or links.
Example (synthetic fixture, not a real supplier statistic):

  id: peak_amendment_available
  type: conditional_probability
  context: {season: peak, days_until_departure: [0, 7]}
  value: 0.80
  approved_range: [0.70, 0.90]
  conditioned_on: request_amendment
  source: synthetic_demo_fixture_v1
  evidence_status: synthetic
  approval_status: approved_for_demo

Approval of an estimate is NOT statistical validation. Public data may inform
priors, but does not establish a particular supplier's reliability. Missing
values should be marked UNKNOWN, never silently replaced with 0 or an LLM
invented number. Separate demo approval from production approval.

5.3 Minimal data set
Create a fictional agency with 2-3 packages, bookings, dated resource slots,
supplier amendment policies, refund/compensation terms and current costs.
Provide 2-3 seasonal contexts (normal/peak and lead-time buckets), conditional
supplier success/failure probabilities and monetized recovery consequences.
Include a business owner utility profile with reference baseline, minimum
acceptable utility gain, risk aversion and valuation horizon. Use one currency.

5.4 Consequence library (not assertion library)
Examples: supplier amendment succeeds/fails; price surcharge; inability to
fulfil; compensation/refund; customer churn; legal claim; payment default.
For every event define trigger, conditional context, outcome branches,
probability references, state effects, economic deltas, downstream events,
and applicability/bounds. Example:

  event: service_failure
  trigger: committed_service_unavailable
  branches:
    - outcome: recovery_required
      probability_ref: p_recovery_given_failure
      effects: [record_recovery, add_loss(recovery_cost)]
    - outcome: no_recovery
      probability_ref: complement
  downstream: [customer_retention_outcome, legal_claim_outcome]

This is a schema sketch: downstream event semantics must specify whether they
are sequential, mutually exclusive or jointly possible. Do not imply they
are independent unless the approved knowledge explicitly says so. Encode
conditional probabilities and shared causes in state. Define whether a
consequence accrues once or per occurrence. Avoid counting a refund as both
lost revenue and an additional loss. Future lost business should use a bounded
horizon and contribution/profit estimate, not gross sales double-counted.

5.5 Coverage and human review
For each operation, maintain an approved list of required risk families and
causal dependencies (cost, availability, cash/payment, fulfilment, liability,
retention, opportunity cost). Mark a family covered, explicitly not applicable
with justification, or unresolved. Recursively expand known applicable events.
Unresolved material risks, missing estimates, expired evidence or operation
semantics outside the pack produce NEEDS_REVIEW. Approved exclusions and
bounded horizons are logged. This is a coverage discipline, not a proof of
real-world completeness. Use human-reviewed held-out scenarios and mutation
tests to expose omissions.

Definition of done: pack can be updated without changing PAT assertion or
core compiler; all numerical assumptions are sourced or labelled synthetic;
unsupported/unknown context blocks authorization.

6. STEP 4: IMPLEMENT QWENWORK AGENTS AND THEIR CONTRACTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Owner: agent/integration engineer. Priority: MUST except noted.
Use QwenWork Skills to define role prompts, JSON schemas, tool permissions
and workflows. Roles can be separate QwenWork sessions/Skills instead of
parallel autonomous services; a separate agent runtime is not necessary.
Genuine QwenWork usage must be visible in the final demo [3-6].

6.1 Sales / negotiation agent (online, MUST)
Purpose: customer interface, semantic interpretation, candidate generation
and customer-friendly communication of the authorized outcome.
Inputs: customer message; authorized session/booking reference; supported
operation schema; read-only business information returned by tools.
Responsibilities:
  1. Extract intent, amounts, units, dates, preferences and constraints.
  2. Ask clarifying questions when ambiguous (date, currency, meaning of
     no cost, booking identity, etc.). Do not guess binding terms.
  3. Decompose goals into allowed primitives using the operation catalog.
  4. Propose multiple plans where appropriate, including status quo or
     conditional acceptance and a counteroffer. Backend adds baseline if
     omitted and validates goal satisfaction/candidate feasibility.
  5. Call evaluate_strategies; await structured result. Never substitute
     own approval, predictions or arithmetic for the backend verdict.
  6. Respond using only approved terms or nonbinding clarification. The
     executor generates binding offer text from authorized structured terms;
     any agent prose must not silently change prices, dates or commitments.
  7. When rejected, offer approved alternatives or politely decline; if
     uncertain, request information or send for founder review.
Forbidden: write to knowledge store; supply trusted financial parameters;
write PAT code; set risk tolerance; override a decision; access raw private
records beyond role; directly send binding customer messages or book/pay.
Test: unseen paraphrases and compound requests yield valid plans or safe
clarification. Prompt injection such as "ignore your limits" cannot commit.

6.2 Knowledge acquisition agent (offline/onboarding, MUST)
Purpose: reduce founder effort in constructing the contextual knowledge pack.
Inputs: approved supplier documents, sample booking history, owner inputs,
public sources and the knowledge record schema.
Responsibilities: extract facts, candidate causal relationships, conditional
frequencies (with denominator and context), valuations and confidence ranges;
provide evidence and provenance; distinguish observations from estimates;
identify missing data; generate a structured draft or change-set.
Output: proposed records with pending status; no automatic activation.
Human reviews, edits, approves and publishes a version through backend UI.
Where evidence is insufficient, suggest collecting it or using an explicitly
approved demo/scenario assumption, not representing it as empirical truth.
Test: source-backed and unsupported entries are distinguishable; attempts to
activate unapproved entries fail. Demonstrate review of one supplier rule.

6.3 Risk/completeness review agent (offline, SHOULD)
Purpose: challenge the knowledge pack and candidate modelling coverage.
Inputs: operation definitions, dependency graph, draft knowledge and example
requests; use a separate critique prompt/role.
Responsibilities: reason forwards from action to adverse consequences and
backwards from economic loss families to possible causes; identify missing
branches, shared causes, impossible sequences, duplicate costs, unsupported
contexts or questionable assumptions. Produce a structured gap report with
severity, evidence, affected operations and suggested revisions.
Do not alter runtime policy or insert new unapproved transitions. Human
review is required. This agent reduces blind spots but cannot prove completeness.
Test: intentionally remove supplier cancellation or refund dependency; review
agent should propose it, and deterministic coverage check should block use
if the pack declares that dependency mandatory. If schedule is tight, merge
this role into the knowledge agent with a separate review pass.

6.4 Decision audit agent (online/post-decision, MUST; small component)
Purpose: capture and summarize the observable decision process for the founder.
Inputs: backend audit events, candidate strategies, applied assumptions,
model-checking results, decision rule, execution outcome, approved terms.
Output: structured decision journal and concise explanation with source IDs.
Backend instrumentation, not the agent, is the primary source of audit facts.
The agent summarizes observable actions, tool calls, inputs, alternatives,
results and stated rationale; do NOT request or store hidden chain-of-thought.
No ability to change verdicts, records, probabilities or business state.
If an explanation fails, decision still follows the verifier; retain raw
record and display a deterministic fallback. Avoid private data exposure.
Test: reconstruct why a request was approved/rejected from log IDs and verify
that cited numbers equal the recorded values. No fabricated explanations.

6.5 Suggested QwenWork connector tools (names illustrative)
- get_operation_catalog(): schema + supported operations (read only).
- get_booking_context(booking_ref): minimal authorized data (read only).
- evaluate_strategies(request_json): validation, compilation, PAT, verdict.
- get_authorized_response(decision_id): approved wording/terms (session bound).
- submit_knowledge_draft(records): create pending records, no publication.
- get_decision_audit(decision_id): role-restricted, redacted evidence.
- submit_risk_review(gaps): create pending review items (optional).
Privileged commit and publish_knowledge operations are backend/human controlled,
not exposed as unrestricted sales-agent tools. Expose MCP JSON schemas and
return typed errors. Verify custom connector transport in the actual workspace;
never assume an undocumented QwenWork webhook, multi-agent API or hook exists.

7. STEP 5: IMPLEMENT ABSTRACT TRANSITION SEMANTICS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Owner: compiler engineer. Priority: MUST.

A transition is not a natural-language intent. It is a parameterized
operation/event with explicit preconditions, possible successors, state
updates and economic effects. The same semantics apply to many customer asks.
Formal model: M = (S, s0, A, P, R), where S is finite state, s0 current state,
A controlled operations, P conditional transition probabilities, and R signed
financial increments. A candidate strategy fixes the agent's choices; external
outcomes remain probabilistic or explicitly nondeterministic.

7.1 Operation template contract
Each operation includes: name/version; typed arguments; preconditions;
read/write dependencies; required data refs; outcomes and probability refs;
effects; emitted causal events; terminal/recovery rules; permitted context.
Use a safe template AST or typed declarative expressions, not eval() or raw
CSP# snippets from untrusted documents. Template authors are trusted engineers.
Suggested examples:
- ADJUST_PRICE (sets authorized proposed price; incremental discount/cost).
- REQUEST_AMENDMENT (supplier response: success, surcharge, unavailable).
- ACQUIRE_RESOURCE / RELEASE_RESOURCE (availability, fees, reservation status).
- CONFIRM_AMENDMENT / KEEP_BOOKING (commit/fallback with accurate state).
- TRANSFER_FUNDS or ISSUE_REFUND (payment success/failure, cost/revenue).
- CREATE_COMMITMENT / SETTLE (if needed to express controlled completion).
MVP implement only enough primitives for discount, amendment and a third
request (e.g. upgrade or compound request); more are optional.

7.2 Environmental events and causal composition
For example, REQUEST_AMENDMENT may return a confirmed hold, unavailable or
additional fee. A premature commitment can lead to service_failure, followed
by refund/recovery, churn or legal claim; each event updates the state and
value. Conditional acceptance instead tests an observed response and chooses
an authorized fallback. The compiler, not the LLM, instantiates these branches.
If one event affects later outcomes (peak season, loss of original reservation),
preserve that dependency using state, not independently sampled marginals.
Bind state transitions to real/simulated API behaviour and distinguish between
reservation check, binding hold, and irrevocable booking.

7.3 Economic accounting and utility
Use V = sum of monetary increments over a clearly defined bounded horizon.
Prefer incremental cash contribution/value relative to a common starting state
for all candidate strategies, with consistent sunk costs and opportunity costs.
For amendments compare against KEEP_BOOKING, not a new sale or arbitrary zero.
For a loss, either reverse revenue or book the refund as a loss, not both.
Translate churn to approved lost contribution/retention cost (bounded horizon).
Use fixed-point integer units and explicit rounding; do not confuse gross
revenue, profit, cash balance, liabilities and economic value.

Utility is a configurable function U(V), supplied by the owner and stored in
a validated profile. MVP example: U(V)=V for V>=0, U(V)=k*V for V<0, k>=1
(e.g. k=3). Values and U bounds must be checked. This is a simple asymmetric
loss-aversion model, not a comprehensive model of insolvency or expected
wealth. Optional: utility based on ending liquidity/wealth, step penalty for
loss exceeding capital or bounded piecewise-linear utility with stated units.
Do not claim positive expected utility guarantees no loss. Legally prohibited,
unauthorized or unsupported actions are excluded by separate execution and
capability checks, not purchasable with sufficiently high expected returns.
These are eligibility checks, not new economic PAT assertions.

Definition of done: manually trace each operation, its possible successors,
and economic increments; same pack supports combinations of primitives; no
new request-specific PAT assertions or external ad hoc valuations.

8. STEP 6: BUILD THE MODEL VERIFICATION COMPILER (CORE DELIVERABLE)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Owner: compiler engineer + verification engineer. Priority: MUST.

8.1 Compiler pipeline
  Input: validated Plan IR, trusted state snapshot, approved knowledge pack,
         utility profile, fixed evaluation horizon, parameter scenario.
  Output: PCSP model, model manifest, model-to-domain event map, utility
          bounds/normalization constants and provenance.

Implement the following ordered passes:
  1. Parse/type-check IR. Resolve supported actions and predicate IDs.
  2. Bind references to a trusted snapshot (booking, supplier terms, dates,
     resource IDs and price). Check customer consent and action eligibility.
  3. Compute dependency closure over operation reads/writes, trigger rules,
     downstream event dependencies and approved required risk families. Stop
     on an unresolved dependency or unsupported semantics. Record the closure.
  4. Instantiate parameterized transition templates. Resolve all numerical
     parameters from the approved context; never from the agent's proposal.
  5. Compose sequential/conditional actions with environmental branches into
     a bounded transition graph. Include the status quo and recovery paths.
     Track event occurrence to prevent duplicate application, and preserve
     dependency state for correlated or conditional events.
  6. Validate the composed graph: reachable state/transition integrity, all
     local probabilities valid and normalized, no unhandled outcomes, finite
     plan, no unintended deadlocks, utility bounded, every possible path
     reaches a defined terminal outcome. A timeout or failed operation needs
     an explicit terminal/fallback interpretation. Do not discard bad paths.
  7. Attach incremental monetary effects, compute terminal value V and
     terminal utility U(V); determine conservative utility bounds L and H.
  8. Lower to PCSP, with synthetic economic-success transition per terminal
     outcome (section 8.3). Generate exactly one fixed runtime assertion.
  9. Store model text, graph/event-ID map, context/assumption IDs, bounds,
     compiler version and content hash; send artifact to the PAT runner.

The model is generated by deterministic software. There must be no free-form
LLM generation of model semantics or assertions at runtime. Identical inputs
and software versions shall produce the same canonical model and manifest.

8.2 Graph lowering recommendation for the one-week MVP
Use a finite acyclic graph as the compiler's internal verification IR.
Each node is a typed business state plus a control location; each edge is a
registered event with probability/choice and economic effect. Enumerate or
memoize reachable states and emit one named PCSP process per reachable node.
Partition outgoing edges into fixed deterministic, stochastic or explicitly
nondeterministic groups. For a fixed strategy, conditional decisions follow
actual observations, not future knowledge; resolve all agent-choice nodes.
Encode environmental uncertainty using approved pcase weights. Group edges
by conditional context so dependent events are not accidentally independent.

For MVP, use an acyclic graph and path/state limits. If those limits are
exceeded, abstain, never prune high-loss paths to manufacture a good score.
Static unrolling permits literal integer pcase weights, even if the tested
PAT version does not support dynamic weight expressions. In a later version,
compile compositional templates directly into symbolic CSP processes to reduce
explicit graph generation overhead. The MVP still runs PAT for the actual
probability result; the state generator is not the authorization oracle.

8.3 Single fixed utility assertion: expected utility via reachability
----------------------------------------------------------------------
PAT PCSP documents probabilistic reachability (pmin/pmax/prob) and weighted
pcase, but not a native expected-utility reward query in the cited manual
[7-9]. A simple reachability query on "profit >= 0" measures probability of
profit, NOT expected profit. To retain PAT and a single assertion, use the
following mathematical reduction.

Let X = U(V_terminal) be terminal utility, bounded by L <= X <= H.
Use common bounds across the candidate strategies in a single decision when
practical; store bounds in the manifest. If H > L, add a synthetic final
Bernoulli step at EVERY terminal business outcome:

             q(X) = (X - L) / (H - L)

  terminal outcome X -- probability q(X) --> economicSuccess = true
                     -- probability 1-q --> economicSuccess = false

The synthetic step is not a real-world event. It is a reduction from an
expected-utility query to a probabilistic reachability query. By total
expectation, for any fixed strategy with almost-sure termination:

    P(economicSuccess) = (E[X] - L) / (H - L)
    E[X] = L + (H - L) * P(economicSuccess)

For bounded nondeterministic environmental branches, pmin computes the
minimum expected utility over the schedulers permitted by the model,
assuming all model paths terminate and the same bounds apply. Do not use
pmax to authorize optimistically; fixed agent choices are already encoded
in the candidate strategy. Disclose semantics of remaining nondeterminism;
MVP preferably fixes a strategy and uses only probabilistic environment
branches. A model checker explores this model, not real-world possibilities
outside its assumptions.

All generated production models shall contain the same query:

    #assert Business() reaches economicSuccess with pmin;

Here economicSuccess is a declared boolean/predicate that is initially false
and set only at a synthetic success terminal. Use the same process/predicate
names and assertion text. No separate customerChurn, legalDispute, service
failure or availability assertions at runtime. These are intermediate events
and monetary effects. Extra assertions may be used in isolated compiler test
fixtures only, not as the decision framework.

Encoding: if utility is integer-valued, emit weighted choices with weights
X-L and H-X (reduce by their greatest common divisor); for exact zero/one
probabilities emit deterministic paths, not invalid zero-weight choices.
If H=L, encode a deterministic success terminal and have the decoder return
L regardless of probability; retain a single fixed query. Reject invalid
bounds or weights exceeding supported PAT integer sizes. Use explicit fixed-
point rounding when required and account for approximation error.

Illustrative PCSP output structure (NOT executed, test syntax in installed PAT):

    var economicSuccess = false;
    Business() = pcase {
        9 : Outcome900()
        1 : Failure()
    };
    Failure() = pcase {
        99 : Outcome750()
        1 : OutcomeLoss()
    };
    // X is scaled to integer utility units; bounds chosen by compiler.
    // L=-999250, H=900, risk-neutral U for this example.
    Outcome900() = success {economicSuccess=true;} -> Skip;
    Outcome750() = pcase {
        20000 : Good()
        3 : Bad()
    };
    OutcomeLoss() = Bad();
    Good() = success {economicSuccess=true;} -> Skip;
    Bad() = no_success -> Skip;
    #assert Business() reaches economicSuccess with pmin;

IMPORTANT: the above graph is a small illustrative skeleton. For Outcome750
with these bounds the exact weights are 1000000 and 150, reduced to 20000:3.
Use the exact fixture in section 12 to verify the generated weights.
All generated code, including PCSP process syntax and pcase format, must be
smoke-tested against the installed version. The manifest records the synthetic
edge separately from real economic events so XAI never describes q as real risk.

8.4 Probability precision is a release-critical issue
The PAT 3.5 manual states that its default printed precision is five decimal
places and value iteration uses a configurable stopping threshold [9]. When
utility bounds differ by millions, rounding a probability can translate into
large monetary error. If the probability error is bounded by delta_p, the
resulting utility error is at most (H-L)*delta_p, plus known input/utility
quantization error. Printed rounding alone is not a certified solver error
bound; a convergence tolerance alone does not necessarily certify one either.

Requirements:
- Inspect actual console output/configuration and whether precision can be
  safely increased in the selected headless setup. Do not assume GUI options
  propagate to CLI or treat decimal output as exact.
- Test near-threshold, rare-event, very large-loss and large-range fixtures.
- For finite acyclic MVP models, implement an independent exact-rational
  evaluation of the compiled graph as a test oracle and runtime precision
  cross-check. At stochastic nodes take the weighted sum of successor values;
  at environmental nondeterministic nodes take minimum. At terminal nodes
  return actual utility. This verifies the reduction / numerical result for
  the graph, not completeness or full compiler correctness.
- Compare PAT reconstructed score against the oracle within a documented
  numeric tolerance. Authorize only if both agree sufficiently and the
  conservative decision margin clears the threshold. If error cannot be
  bounded, consult exact graph result for a conservative cross-check but mark
  discrepancy or insufficient solver precision as review, never PAT-verified.
- If the PAT integration fails, use the graph oracle only as an explicitly
  labelled development fallback; do not falsely present it as actual PAT use.
- If expected utility cannot be obtained reliably using PAT, document the
  blocker and consider a native reward backend as future work (e.g. PRISM).
  The primary demo must use PAT for actual evaluated decisions.

8.5 Parameter uncertainty
For MVP, run the same fixed assertion over an explicitly enumerated set of
approved parameter scenarios (e.g. normal and adverse supplier conditions).
Store the same strategy across scenarios; do not choose a different plan
with knowledge of hidden future conditions. Compare the minimum scenario
utility and report sensitivity. This is robustness across the enumerated set,
not a proof for every point of a continuous interval. A sound interval model
or validated extrema argument is required for full interval guarantees.
Do not generate independent worst-case probabilities for separate adverse
outcomes; all event propagation and rewards are evaluated jointly within
one model. Avoid assuming independent events without evidence.

8.6 Single verification objective versus implementation safeguards
The sole economic criterion is expected utility (or worst expected utility
across the approved scenarios) relative to the reference and threshold.
Identity, customer consent, legal/capability eligibility, freshness and input
validation are separate deterministic pre-execution requirements; they must
not be traded for money. Structural tests enforce model well-formedness, not
new per-request business assertions. Account for uncertainty by abstention,
not by accepting a superficially profitable but incompletely modelled action.

Definition of done: generated models for discounts, rescheduling and compound
requests all contain the exact same fixed PAT assertion; utility results
match independent known values; omitted required risk/context or invalid
arithmetic rejects before authorization; actual PAT runs are reproducible.

9. STEP 7: IMPLEMENT PAT ADAPTER AND DECISION ORCHESTRATOR
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Owner: verification/backend engineer. Priority: MUST.

9.1 Runner
- Use one isolated temporary directory per run, safe filenames, fixed tool
  binary and module selection, subprocess argument list, no shell injection.
- For PAT3.Console.exe, test the installed command, typically:
    PAT3.Console.exe -pcsp model.csp results.txt
  Confirm exact extension/argument order using installed help. Record version
  and module. Retain stdout/stderr and output files for debugging.
- Enforce timeout, memory/state limits and bounded concurrent jobs. A zero
  process exit code with empty results is not success. Verify the assertion
  text, expected result format, status and numeric field explicitly; do not
  parse an unrelated printed number as probability. Reject NaN, missing,
  out-of-range and incomplete results. Store duration and state count if
  provided by the actual tool. Redact sensitive content and avoid secrets.
- Return typed status SUCCESS / TIMEOUT / PARSE_ERROR / TOOL_ERROR /
  NUMERIC_UNCERTAIN; only SUCCESS plus validated score may authorize.

9.2 Decision engine
For each feasible plan evaluate utility including rejection/status quo.
Let score(plan) = minimum expected utility over approved parameter scenarios.
Compare consistently against a configurable owner threshold and baseline;
recommended MVP rule: approve a customer-satisfying strategy only if its
conservative score is at least baseline score + minimum gain and any absolute
utility floor configured by owner. If score uncertainty overlaps the threshold
or candidate ranking is unresolved, request review or choose a clearly safe
nonbinding alternative. Do not round before making the decision.
For an already booked trip, rejecting an amendment may itself create churn;
model that consequence in the baseline rather than silently assigning zero.
An alternative that needs customer consent is a COUNTEROFFER, not a commit.
If no strategy qualifies, reject/clarify/escalate with an appropriate response.
Score is conditional on selected model, horizon, known probabilities and owner
utility; not guaranteed profit or guarantee against all business risk.

9.3 Authorization/execution
Create an expiring, one-use, signed or server-stored authorization linking
session/customer ID, strategy ID, exact approved terms, business snapshot
version, knowledge and utility versions, decision ID and model hash.
For a contingent plan, enforce only the verified next operation conditional
on actual observed state; never let an authorization to ask a supplier become
an authorization to promise delivery. Re-evaluate if new supplier information
or state invalidates the verified assumptions. Check token expiry, replay,
identity and state version atomically with the sandbox commit (idempotency
key). A changed price/date, stale state, missing token or direct write must
be rejected. Simulate actual booking/quote transitions for the MVP.

Definition of done: verifier cannot be bypassed via the execution API;
reject, error, stale snapshot and unauthorized tool outputs cannot commit.

10. STEP 8: AUDITABILITY AND EXPLAINABLE AI (XAI)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Owner: backend + audit/agent engineer. Priority: MUST; keep compact.

10.1 Audit trail (deterministic instrumentation)
Use an append-only event table or JSONL journal with access control. Log:
- timestamp, correlation ID, actor/role, request ID and (redacted) request;
- extracted goal, customer constraints, candidate plan(s), validation result;
- source references, assumptions, context/date, approval and pack versions;
- compiler graph/model hash, operation IDs, utility and bounds, scenario IDs;
- PAT assertion, probability result, solver version, precision, duration,
  status and oracle check; retain model artifact for authorized reviewer;
- comparison by candidate, threshold/baseline, numerical decision rationale;
- verdict, exact approved terms, authorization events, customer response and
  execution result or reason for abstention.
Record observable tool calls and structured decision traces, not hidden model
reasoning. Store minimally necessary personal data; access and retention policy
are necessary for later production. Do not call a simple local append-only
log tamper-proof; hash chaining is optional, not a substitute for access control.

10.2 XAI tool
Primary explanation is derived from verifier data and model provenance, not
an LLM guessing what happened. Show:
  (a) request interpretation and strategy considered;
  (b) baseline and candidate expected utilities with units and margins;
  (c) important modelled causal paths and economic drivers;
  (d) sensitivity to approved parameter scenarios and utility settings;
  (e) reason for approve/counteroffer/reject/escalate and approved alternative;
  (f) evidence/assumption status (synthetic, estimate, observed) and limits.
For the small acyclic graph, compute path probabilities and terminal utility
contributions using a deterministic forward traversal or dynamic programming
under the same fixed strategy. These are diagnostics from the model, NOT extra
PAT assertions. Do not display independent pmin/pmax paths as one actual
probability distribution. If nondeterminism remains and no matching policy is
available, show feasible example paths/intervals only or label the limitation.
Example: baseline profit 900, expected retention loss 15, expected legal loss
1000, expected value -115 under the illustrative risk-neutral profile. With
loss-aversion, distinguish expected value from expected utility. Use terminal
contributions or clearly label an exact monetary decomposition versus a
nonlinear utility decomposition. Do not double count overlapping consequences.
Optional counterfactual explanation: re-evaluate same query with ONE approved
parameter or action changed, e.g. amendment with a confirmed supplier hold.
Report the change as a model-based sensitivity, not causal proof from data.

10.3 Audit agent behaviour
Read-only QwenWork audit Skill creates a founder-friendly summary citing event
IDs and numeric fields. Backend validates numbers and known references against
journal; if invalid, show deterministic report. It shall not overwrite recorded
facts, trade-offs or decision status. Customer-facing explanation must omit
private supplier margins, sensitive legal assumptions and internal records;
show permitted terms and a polite high-level reason instead.

Definition of done: founder can click a decision and reconstruct the chain
from request -> plan -> approved assumptions -> model -> PAT -> utility ->
verdict -> action. A changed audit-agent summary cannot alter the outcome.

12. STEP 10: TESTING AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Owner: all, with separate reviewer for verification. Priority: MUST.

12.1 Numerical oracle fixtures (exact, synthetic)
A. Simple fixed discount: price 120, cost 90, requested discount 10; net value
   20 for the offered new sale. If the comparison baseline is no transaction
   with value 0 and utility is identity, score=20. Negative profit requests
   should be rejected against that baseline. Distinguish price from profit.
B. Rare loss: terminal profit $900 (p=.9), $750 (p=.099), -$999,250 (p=.001).
   E[V]=-$115 exactly; risk-neutral bounds L=-999250, H=900. Expected
   synthetic probability = (999250-115)/1000150 = 999135/1000150.
   Terminal weights for $750 are 1,000,000 and 150 (reduced 20000:3).
   With U(V)=V if V>=0 and 3V otherwise, E[U]=-2113.50. Values use same
   synthetic units; real implementation should use signed cents consistently.
   These event probabilities and financial losses are invented demo inputs,
   not evidence about Mesho or any actual travel operator.
C. P(profit)>0 test: 99% chance of $100 gain and 1% chance of $100,000 loss
   produces E[V]=-$901 despite 99% profitability probability. PAT query must
   recover expected utility, not approve simply because likely profitable.
D. Deterministic cases incl. H=L, q=0/q=1, all-negative outcomes, zero net
   payoff, rare event, near-threshold precision and very large utility range.
E. Conditional consequences: legal dispute conditional on service failure;
   compare exact terminal joint distribution. Independent marginals should
   not be substituted. Test repeated costs and overlapping refund events.

12.2 Flagship travel decision (incremental, same starting booking)
Customer asks: "Can I move my existing booking 30 days later at no extra cost?"
Use common starting state and illustrative probabilities/valuations. Example
relative to keeping the original booking BEFORE considering customer reaction:
  - Reject: expected incremental retention loss -$150 (fixture deterministic).
  - Guarantee immediately: 80% chance amendment succeeds at extra $80 fee;
    19.8% chance delivery failure costs $150; 0.2% chance delivery failure
    causes $150 cost AND $1,000,000 lawsuit. E[V]=-2094.
  - Conditional amendment: 80% chance supplier binding confirmation at $80
    cost; 20% unavailable, preserve original, retention cost $150. E[V]=-$94.
  - Optional paid alternative: model the probability of consenting and fees;
    not an approved commitment until consent is obtained.
These are illustrative paths and must be refined if real supplier conditions
require other costs/events. Under risk-neutral utility, the conditional plan
improves on rejection by $56 while the immediate unconditional plan is worse.
A configured absolute floor of zero on incremental outcome would reject all
negative plans; set floor/threshold appropriately for a service-recovery demo
(e.g. compare against status quo and require gain >= 0, with no separate zero
floor). Under loss-aversion, evaluate the same function for every candidate.
Use the exact graph oracle to calculate expected results; never hardcode
these into the actual verdict. Conditional execution may authorize a supplier
request only; it confirms the new date only after the hold is verified.

12.3 End-to-end held-out suite
Prepare at least 12-20 requests; freeze some examples until after development.
Include paraphrases, ambiguous dates, $ versus %, discount, reschedule,
upgrade, compound request, out-of-scope loyalty conversion, unsupported
supplier policy, missing probability, stale supplier/booking version, failed
PAT job and malicious attempt to override policy. Add tests changing the
approved probability/valuation and checking the expected score changes.
Use reviewer-labelled reference plans and consequences. Count supported,
correctly clarified, correctly abstained and incorrectly authorized cases.
Compare with unrestricted agent and simple fixed-rule baseline if feasible;
judge against the same synthetic scenarios and utility profile. Report actual
measured results only, with test sample sizes and limitations.

12.4 Coverage and enforcement test checklist
- New approved risk/valuation extends knowledge without changing query text.
- New combination of known operations compiles without new request handler.
- Missing required risk family, unapproved fact, out-of-range parameter or
  arbitrary operation causes NEEDS_REVIEW/ERROR rather than guessed value.
- Probabilities normalized; all terminal paths counted; overflow guarded.
- PAT runner catches empty output even with exit 0, timeout and parse mismatch.
- Numeric cross-check matches fixtures, no borderline approvals.
- A rejected or stale authorization cannot execute; wrong session cannot use
  token; repeated use is idempotent or rejected; unapproved terms cannot send.
- Audit explanation cites recorded assumptions and results; no fabricated facts.
- QwenWork genuinely used in demo; Hashmeta AI website published and linked.

Performance target (proposed): bounded small-model negotiation evaluation
within ~10 seconds on the demo machine where practical; timeout/abstain if
not feasible. Record real latency instead of promising it. Other MVP targets:
all numerical fixtures pass; zero unauthorized commits in specified tests;
all held-out requests report a valid supported outcome or explicit abstention.
Do not describe these targets as achieved until actually measured.

15. LIMITATIONS AND CLAIM BOUNDARIES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Formal guarantee is relative to composed state/transition model, approved
  assumptions, utility semantics and correctly enforced execution interface.
- Data validity: estimates require evidence and human review, approval alone
  does not establish calibration; demo inputs are not actual industry rates.
- Completeness: no absolute guarantee of all real-world consequences. Risk
  review, dependency closure and held-out scenarios expose known gaps;
  unknown unknowns remain. Record bounded horizon and scope. Fail closed
  on detected gaps, not on an imagined ability to detect all omissions.
- Expected utility expresses owner's trade-offs, not a guarantee of profit,
  solvency, lawful behavior or customer satisfaction. Non-permissible actions
  excluded before evaluation; do not use cost-benefit to legitimize them.
- The proposed one-query reduction is mathematical for bounded terminating
  models; PAT code, actual precision and platform integration are not yet
  executed or validated in this handoff. Require Day 1 technical verification.
- Direct Hashmeta CRM integration, production QwenWork invocation hooks and
  cross-industry generalization are not assumed or claimed. Prototype includes
  mock transaction endpoint; disclose any manual integration step honestly.
